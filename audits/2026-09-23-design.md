# Palimpsest — design and UX audit, 23 September 2026

Phone-first review of the running app at 375×812, light and dark, at the default
reading size (19px) and the largest (26px / 2.0 line spacing / +12% letter
spacing). A round was played end to end, and the answer card, round-end,
Progress, a thread, the library and Settings were walked through in both normal
and quiet mode.

The findings integrated from the September 18 audits (ultraviolet palette,
undertext hero, epicentre strip, sibling type family, round-end "what slipped",
back button, upright quotes, reading settings, sentence read-aloud, glossary on
tap, prompt split) are not repeated. What follows is what is still wrong, most of
it in the seams those changes left behind — and in the new quiet mode, which has
not been laid out for yet.

Measurements are from the live page, not estimated.

---

## Must-fix

### 1. The options ignore the reader's own text size
*app/styles.css:335 (`.opt`), 189 (`.order-truth`), 227 (`.bigs`), 229
(`.voice-day .vq`), 204 (`.lane-label`), 433 (`.mention p`), 424 (`.item b`)*

`.ask`, `.setup`, `.quote` and `.answer` are all sized from `--read-size`. The
answer options are not: `.opt` is hard-set to `500 1.03rem/1.45 var(--ui)`. Set
the reading size to its maximum and the question renders at 27px serif while the
four things you must read and choose between stay at 16.5px sans. The same holds
for Today's voice on Home (fixed 1.24rem), the timeline labels on a thread, the
ordered-answer list, and the library's mention snippets.

**Why it matters.** The reading-size control exists because the owner is
dyslexic. A reader who turns it up is telling the app they cannot comfortably
read at 19px — and the app answers by enlarging the question and leaving the
answers small. It is the one place where getting the text wrong costs a mark.

**Fix.** Drive `.opt` from the reading variables the way `.ask` does:
`font: 500 calc(var(--read-size) * .92)/1.45 var(--read-font); letter-spacing:
var(--read-ls);`. Do the same for `.order-truth`, `.voice-day .vq`,
`.lane-label`, `.bigs` and `.mention p`. Chrome — buttons, labels, counts —
should stay on the UI scale; anything that is prose should not.

### 2. The evidence quote is 21 characters wide
*app/styles.css:325 (`.q-head`), 352 (`.quote`); app/js/app.js:711 (`revealCard`)*

Measured on the answer card at 375px: the key quote box is **253px wide and
21 characters per line**, and a single sentence runs to **10 lines**. The right
answer restated directly above it gets 289px. The quote is the narrowest column
on the screen.

The cause is stacking: `.app` padding 16px + `.card` padding 18px + `.quote`
padding 16px + 3px rule + the `.q-head` grid reserving a fixed `44px` column for
the read-aloud button — 122px of the 375px gone before a word is set.

**Why it matters.** This is the moment the app is built for: the sentence from
the book that proves the answer. Set at 21 characters it reads as rubble, and
the eye slides off it to the "More from the source" link. Evidence set worse
than the interface around it reads as decoration.

**Fix.** Take the read-aloud button out of the quote's grid row — put it on a
`.src-head` line above the quote, as `voice-day` and `entryScreen` already do,
so the quote gets the full column. Then drop `.quote` side padding to 12px under
`@media (max-width: 420px)`. Target: 38–45 characters a line at 19px.

### 3. Quiet mode leaves a 44px dead gutter down every question and option
*app/styles.css:325 (`.q-head`), 330 (`.optrow`)*

Both are `grid-template-columns: 1fr 44px`. In quiet mode `sayBtn()` returns
`null` (ui.js:52) so the second cell is empty, but the column stays. Measured
with quiet on: `.optrow` is 343px, the option button inside it is **293px** — a
50px empty strip runs down the right edge of every option, the question, the
setup line and the quote. The evidence quote is still 253px / 21 characters with
nothing beside it.

**Why it matters.** Quiet mode is for a bus or a café. It is exactly when the
reader has no audio fallback and needs every pixel of line length — and it is
the one mode where the app gives away 15% of the width to nothing.

**Fix.** Either collapse the column when there is one child —
`.q-head:has(> :only-child), .optrow:has(> :only-child) { grid-template-columns:
1fr; }` — or set a `data-quiet` attribute on `<html>` in `applySettings()` and
override the two grids from it. The `:has()` form is better: it also covers the
case where `speechAvailable()` is false on a device with no voices.

### 4. Read-aloud is missing from the app's own prose
*app/js/app.js:790–898 (Progress), 941–988 (About), 156–166 (thread),
735–784 (round-end); app/js/library.js:280 (library index)*

`sayBtn` is wired into quoted passages — the question, the options, the key
quote, today's voice, glossary definitions, library entry leads. It is wired
into none of the app's own writing. Counted on the live page:

| Screen | Height | Read-aloud buttons |
|---|---|---|
| Progress | 5,092px (6.3 screens) | **0** |
| About, sources and licences | 4,316px, 982 words | **0** |
| Thread (intro, 4 big questions, 21 events) | — | **0** |
| Library index | 3,212px | **0** |
| Round-end card | — | **0** |

**Why it matters.** The standing rule is that read-aloud is available for
anything text-heavy. Progress is where the app explains what "known", "met",
"holding" and the 80–85% band mean, and it is the longest screen in the app.
About carries the licensing and the promise about verification. A reader who
uses read-aloud to get through the *sources* is left to grind through the app's
*explanations* unaided.

**Fix.** Add a `sayBtn` to the header row of each explanatory card: the "What you
can answer" card, the ring card's known/met/holding paragraph, the "Reviews,
first try" note, the round-end summary paragraph, the thread's opening
paragraph, and each About section. Cheapest general form: a small helper
`explain(label, ...kids)` that wraps a card, builds its spoken text from the
kids' `textContent`, and hangs one button off the label row.

### 5. Home is a browse screen with two actions bolted on
*app/js/app.js:352–369*

Measured on Home, 375×812, 2,595px tall (3.2 screens):

| | top |
|---|---|
| Hero (undertext, wordmark, tagline, epicentre strip) | 0–372 |
| **Learn** | 388 |
| Today's voice | 484 |
| Ring and stats | 734 |
| Canada — 4 chapter rows | 919 |
| Around the world — 6 chapter rows | 1,340 |
| Threads | 1,932 |
| **Recall test** | 2,100 |
| **Library · Progress · Settings** | 2,266 |
| **I'm in public — go quiet** | 2,363 |
| All the voices · About | 2,437 |

**1,133px — 44% of Home — is browse-everything list sitting between the two
actions.** Recall test is 2.6 screens down. The three navigation tiles are 2.8
screens down. The quiet switch — a control whose whole point is that you reach
for it suddenly, on a bus — is 2.9 screens down.

The standing rule is the opposite: Home biased towards necessary actions, with
browse-everything lists at the foot.

**Why it matters.** A returning player's second-most-likely intent (a recall
test) and their route to everything else are below three screens of chapter
inventory they will scroll past every single day.

**Fix.** Reorder: hero → Learn → Recall test → ring/stats → Today's voice →
tiles → *then* Canada, Around the world, Threads → voices/about. Move the quiet
switch into the hero as a small icon button beside the wordmark (it is a state,
not a link, and it already carries `aria-pressed`). If the chapter lists are
wanted on Home at all, collapse them to a single "Browse by chapter" row that
opens the existing Progress "By chapter" view — they are already duplicated
there.

### 6. Going back from a thread or a round-end lands you on Home
*app/js/app.js:167 `route('thread', homeScreen)`, 785 `route('done', homeScreen)`*

Reproduced: Home → thread "Trade, empire and abolition" → "Where this comes
from" → reader → back → **Home**. The thread screen, the region filter and the
scroll position are gone.

`show('thread', () => threadScreen(t.id))` (app.js:132) passes no `arg`, so
`history.state` carries nothing the route can rebuild from, and the registered
route falls back to `homeScreen`. `route('done', homeScreen)` is the same
pattern: tap a row under "What slipped — read it again", read the passage, press
back, and the round-end summary is gone — including the list of what you missed,
which is the one thing on that screen you came back for.

**Fix.** For the thread: `show('thread', threadScreen, { arg: t.id })` and
`route('thread', threadScreen)`. For the round-end, keep the last finish payload
in a module variable and register `route('done', () => doneScreen(lastFinish))`,
rendering Home only if there is no payload.

### 7. The library's Search tab is off the right edge of the phone
*app/js/library.js:281–284; app/styles.css:417 (`.tabs`)*

Measured: the tab strip's content is **509px** inside a **347px** box. "Read the
books" is clipped at the viewport edge; **"Search" is entirely invisible**. The
strip scrolls, but there is no fade, no arrow and no partial peek of the fourth
tab to say so.

**Why it matters.** Search is the only way into 1,770 passages by anything other
than browsing. It is reachable only by accident — by swiping a strip that does
not look swipeable.

**Fix.** Two rows of two on narrow screens (`flex-wrap: wrap` and let the tabs
fill), or shorten the labels ("Peoples", "Glossary", "Books", "Search") so all
four fit at 375px. If the strip must scroll, add a right-edge mask
(`mask-image: linear-gradient(90deg, #000 90%, transparent)`) so the cut is
visible.

### 8. The whose-history line names nobody for the thread, and says one chapter twice
*app/js/app.js:516–525 (`contextLine`)*

Every question's context line, computed from the live pack:

| Chapter | Line shown |
|---|---|
| Before contact | Before contact · Turtle Island, to about 1450 |
| Amazonia | Amazonia, a centre of its own · The Bolivian Amazon, about 500–1400 CE |
| New France | **New France, inside other worlds · New France, 1530s–1760s** |
| Contact | Contact, on the nations' terms · North America, 1500s–1700s |
| Trade, empire and abolition | **Trade, empire and abolition, 1488–1842 · Four regions, 1488–1842** |
| Africa / China / South Asia / Islamic world / Europe | (de-duplicated correctly) |

Two failures:

- **New France prints its name twice** and the thread prints its date range
  twice. The de-dup at line 522 only fires when the era *starts with the whole
  chapter title*; "New France, 1530s–1760s" does not start with "New France,
  inside other worlds".
- **The 24 thread questions say "Four regions".** Only 22 of 317 questions carry
  a year (`q.at`), so 295 fall back to the chapter era. A thread question about
  Macau shows "Trade, empire and abolition, 1488–1842 · 1557" — the year, and
  nothing about China. The thread already stores a region per timeline event
  (`e.lane`, with names in `t.lanes`); the questions do not use it.

The rule is that every question says whose history it is asking about. At
chapter granularity it is satisfied loosely at best — the Aztec/Tenochtitlan
question is filed under "North America, 1500s–1700s" and never names the Mexica.

**Fix.** (a) Change the de-dup to a token overlap — if the era's first comma-
delimited phrase appears anywhere in the title, drop it. (b) Give thread
questions a `lane`, and build the line from the lane's name plus the year:
"China and Asia · 1557". (c) Where a question is about one named people, let it
carry an explicit `who` that overrides the chapter title.

## Should-fix

### 9. "Questions you get right show up here" sits next to the number of them
*app/js/app.js:88, used at 358 (Home) and 823 (Progress)*

`growthLine()` falls back to *"Questions you get right show up here, and climb
as you learn."* whenever there is no week-on-week gain and nothing turned
around. On a returning player with no snapshot a week back, Home prints
"5 · can answer" and that empty-state sentence beside it; Progress prints
**"5 of 317"** and then the same sentence directly underneath. It reads as "you
have none" against a number that says otherwise.

**Fix.** Only use the empty-state sentence when `can === 0`. Otherwise say
something true of the present: "5 so far — the line starts once you've played
two days."

### 10. "Nothing else is due today" sits above a button offering another round
*app/js/app.js:758 and 778*

The round-end card printed: *"4 of 5 right. You can answer 5 of 317. Nothing
else is due today. The next question comes round Friday."* — with a primary
button below it reading **"Another round"**, because 311 questions are unseen.
`due` (reviews) being zero is not "nothing else". Home then also still offers
"Learn · 5 new questions waiting".

**Fix.** Split the two ideas: "No reviews left today — the next comes round
Friday. There are still N new questions if you want them." Or suppress the
next-due sentence entirely when `fresh > 0` and let the button carry it.

### 11. The round-end does not show that you improved
*app/js/app.js:748–758*

The card gives a score (4 of 5), a cumulative fraction (5 of 317 — 1.6%, a
number that will look almost identical tomorrow), and the next due date.
`growthLine()` can only add "up N on a week ago", which needs a seven-day-old
snapshot, and "N turned around", which needs a miss-then-hit on a later day.
A new or intermittent player gets neither, so the only thing that moves between
rounds is a denominator of 317.

**Fix.** Add the two comparisons that always exist: **"+4 since you opened the
app today"** (from the day record, `days[dayKey()]`) and **"+N since you started
this round"** (`State.canAnswer(round.scope)` captured in `play()` alongside
`heldBefore`). Lead with the one that moved.

### 12. Progress is 6.3 screens, and more than half of it is one list of zeros
*app/js/app.js:849–860*

Measured section heights on Progress (total 5,092px):

| Section | Height |
|---|---|
| What you can answer + chart | 274 |
| Then and now | 139 |
| **The big questions** | **2,810** |
| Ring / known-met-holding | 213 |
| By chapter | 918 |
| Reviews, first try | 114 |
| By thread | 312 |
| Recent days | 88 |

"The big questions" renders every big question of all eleven chapters — 34 rows
— each with a bar and a count, most of them `0/N` on any realistic profile. It
is 55% of the screen and it pushes the two sections a player actually uses to
judge themselves (first-try accuracy, recent days) below 4.5 screens of
scrolling.

**Fix.** Show only chapters the player has met a question in; put the rest
behind "Show every chapter". Move "Reviews, first try" and "Recent days" up,
directly under the growth chart — they are the answer to "am I improving?" and
they are currently last.

### 13. "By thread" on Progress is not what "Threads" means on Home
*app/js/app.js:882*

Home has "Threads across the world" — the cross-region timeline chapters.
Progress has a section called "By thread" listing the five *lenses* ("On its own
terms", "Beyond the myths", "Exchange and obligation", "Still argued", "How the
past is kept"). Two different things, one word, one app.

The counts also do not reconcile: 99 + 58 + 42 + 9 + 177 = **385 of 317
questions**, because a question can carry several lenses, and nothing on the
screen says so.

**Fix.** Rename the Progress section to "Ways of looking" (the word the answer
card's chips already teach) and add one line: "A question can sit under more
than one, so these add up to more than 317."

### 14. Progress's chapter rows silently start a round, then drop you on Home
*app/js/app.js:875 (`chapterRows(chapterStats())`), 483*

`chapterRows` is shared between Home and Progress. On Progress the rows look
like report rows — title, bar, "48 new" — but tapping one starts a round. If you
then leave that round, `leaveSheet` does `show('home', homeScreen, { replace:
true })`: you tapped something on Progress and ended on Home, with Progress gone
from history.

**Fix.** On Progress, render the rows as non-interactive (or have them scroll to
that chapter), and have the leave-round action return to the screen the round
was started from — keep the caller's route name on `round` and `show()` it.

### 15. "More from the source" prints the same citation three times
*app/js/app.js:713–729*

Expanded on one answer card, the disclosure produced, in order: a blockquote of
the "rest" quotes, then — for each of three paragraphs from the same section —
the paragraph, **"Read this section — §5.3 The Widowed Land"**, **"John Douglas
Belshaw, Canadian History: Pre-Confederation (BCcampus, 2015), CC BY 4.0"**, and
the caption "Read aloud, sentence by sentence." The opening blockquote reprinted
sentences that then appeared verbatim in the paragraphs below it.

So: one section, one book, credited three times; one instruction repeated three
times; and the key sentences shown twice.

**Why it matters.** Repeated boilerplate is how a citation stops reading as
proof and starts reading as a licence notice. The rigour is real — it should not
look like paperwork.

**Fix.** Group the paragraphs by section. Print `citeLine` and the full `e.cite`
once, at the foot of the group. Drop the "rest" blockquote when its sentences
already occur in a paragraph shown below. Show the "Read aloud, sentence by
sentence" caption once, above the group.

### 16. The citation on the answer card is not a link
*app/js/app.js:731*

The closed answer card ends the key quote with `h('p', { class: 'cite' },
'§5.3')` — plain text. The tappable version (`citeLine`, app.js:377) only exists
*inside* the disclosure. So the one visible piece of provenance on the card
cannot be followed; you must open "More from the source" to find a way in.

**Fix.** Use `citeLine(key)` on the closed card — "Read this section — §5.3 The
Widowed Land" — and let "More from the source" carry the other quotes and the
full paragraph.

### 17. "Leave" is the primary button in the leave-round sheet
*app/js/app.js:481–483*

The destructive choice is the filled accent button; "Keep going" is the grey
one. Screenshotted at the largest reading size, where the sheet also covers the
remaining options.

**Fix.** Swap them: `Keep going` primary, `Leave` plain. The heading already
asks the question.

### 18. The context line is all-caps, repeats on every card, and breaks "1500s"
*app/styles.css:324 (`.where`); app/js/app.js:516*

`text-transform: uppercase` at `.78rem` with `letter-spacing: .05em`. Three
consequences seen on the phone:

- Word shape is destroyed — the one typographic choice dyslexia guidance is
  unanimous about, in an app that otherwise gets type right.
- The ordinal breaks: **"NORTH AMERICA, 1500S–1700S"**.
- At 59–70 characters it wraps to two lines (three at the largest reading size)
  and leaves the "·" dangling at a line end.
- Within a chapter round it is byte-identical on all five cards, so it carries
  no information after the first — it is 55px of repeated shouting above every
  question.

**Fix.** Drop the uppercase; set it as sentence case at `.82rem`, `600`,
`--ink-3`, no letter-spacing. If it should still read as a label rather than
prose, use a small leading dot or a rule instead of caps. Consider showing it
once at the head of a chapter round and only on the cards where it changes.

### 19. The thread timeline is a column of 17 identical links
*app/js/app.js:147–150*

Every timeline row whose paragraph is in the library gets a **"Where this comes
from"** button — 17 of them on the one thread, all the same accent-coloured
text, all with the same accessible name. The rows whose source is *not* in the
library instead print the full citation inline ("Anders Ljungstedt, *An
Historical Sketch…*, Public domain"). Two visual treatments in one list, chosen
by a data accident.

**Why it matters.** The repeated link is the most visually prominent thing in
the timeline, so the eye tracks boilerplate instead of dates and events. For a
screen reader it is 17 buttons with no distinguishing name.

**Fix.** Make the whole row the button and mark it with a small `§` or chevron
at the right; put the citation (link or text, uniformly) in the row's small
print. If the link must stay separate, name it for the row: `Where "${e.label}"
comes from`.

### 20. Three places tell you there is more and give you no way to see it
*app/js/library.js:408 (entry mentions), 314 (glossary), 359/366 (search)*

- An entry prints six mentions per chapter then **"…and 3 more in this
  chapter."** with no control. The remaining passages are unreachable.
- The glossary lists `.slice(0, 120)` rows while the caption underneath states
  the *full* count ("N definitions, from the chapter summaries of both books") —
  so you are told there are more than are shown, silently.
- Search stops at 40 passages and says "First 40 passages" with no way to see
  the rest and no suggestion to narrow the query.

**Fix.** Give each a "Show the rest (N)" button that re-renders with the full
list; for search, add "Add a word to narrow it."

### 21. Chips are 36px targets
*app/styles.css:361*

`.chip { min-height: 36px }` — below the 44px minimum the September audit set,
and everything else in the app honours (`.icon`, `.disclose`, `.item`, `.seg
button`, `.tabs button`, `.chrow` are all 44px or more). The chips are the
thread's region filter and the answer card's lens buttons.

**Fix.** `min-height: 44px; padding: 0 14px;`.

### 22. At the largest reading size the fourth option is below the fold, unsignalled
*app/js/app.js:527–532; app/styles.css:325–328*

At 26px / 2.0 / +12%, a two-sentence prompt occupies about 500px of the 812px
viewport (setup 3 lines, ask 6 lines). Only two and a half of the four options
are visible, and nothing indicates there are four — no count, no fade, no
partial row by design.

**Fix.** Cap the prompt block at roughly `55dvh` with a scroll of its own, or —
simpler and better for this reader — print "Four answers" as the options'
heading so the number is stated before the scroll.

### 23. The setup line and the restated answer ignore the letter-spacing preference
*app/styles.css:326 (`.setup`), 349 (`.answer`)*

`.ask`, `.quote` and `.reading` all carry `letter-spacing: var(--read-ls)`;
`.setup` and `.answer` do not. At +12% the effect is plainly visible: the
question is widely spaced, the sentence that sets it up is not, and neither is
the right answer when you miss.

**Fix.** Add `letter-spacing: var(--read-ls);` to both rules.

### 24. The quiet switch is 2.9 screens down Home
*app/js/app.js:367–371*

Covered under finding 5, repeated here because it is the one control whose value
is entirely in how fast you can reach it. It is also conditional — it only
appears at all if `quiet || sound || readAloud === 'auto'` — so a player who
turns sound off in Settings loses the switch from Home without being told.

**Fix.** Move it into the hero as an icon toggle, always present.

## Nice-to-have

### 25. The undertext is clipped mid-word
*app/styles.css:154–158*

Measured: each of the three ghost lines has a `scrollWidth` of 1,147–1,287px in
a 351px box — roughly **70% of every line is cut off**, so all three end
mid-word ("…written by an Indian histor", "…the Six Nations were a", "I have
been a slave myself—I know"). It is `aria-hidden` and `pointer-events: none`, so
it is decoration by intent, but it is legible-enough decoration made of three
truncated sentences at the very top of the app — visual noise in the one place a
dyslexic reader most wants calm, and the third line is the app's most affecting
quotation, amputated.

**Fix.** Either fade each line out at its own right edge with a per-line
horizontal mask so no line ends on a broken word, or choose phrases short enough
to fit at 375px.

### 26. Dark mode separates the option cards with a shadow
*app/styles.css:331–336; 60–78 (dark tokens)*

`.opt` is `--surface #1C1926` on `--ground #121019` with `--shadow: 0 8px 24px
rgba(0,0,0,.3)` — a black shadow on a near-black page does nothing. The cards
are distinguishable but only just, and that is the entire visual signal that
they are tappable.

**Fix.** In dark, give `.opt`, `.card` and `.mode` a `1px solid var(--line)`
border, or lift `--surface-2` by a few points and use it for the option's
resting state.

### 27. The right answer is printed twice on a miss
*app/js/app.js:709*

When you are wrong the card restates the right answer in large serif — directly
below the option list where that same answer is already marked with a ✓ and a
green ground. On a phone the repeat costs about 90px and pushes the evidence
further under the sticky "Next".

**Fix.** Keep the restatement only where the option list is not on screen (order
questions), or shorten it to a lead-in for the quote: "Because —".

### 28. The round meter does not move until the next question
*app/js/app.js:495–503*

`topBar()` is built once per question from `tally.n`, so after you answer
question 1 the bar still reads 0% and the counter still says "1 of 5" while you
read the answer card. The step happens only when the next card renders.

**Fix.** Advance the meter in `grade()`, or render the counter as "1 of 5
answered" so the reading is unambiguous.

### 29. Small navigation leftovers

- **Round-end** opens with an empty `.topbar` (app.js:737) — 56px of nothing
  where a back affordance would sit.
- **Library entry screens** have a topbar with a back button and no title
  (library.js:392), unlike every other screen.
- **Faded options** get no `aria-label` update after an answer (app.js:544), so
  a screen reader hears them as ordinary unlabelled choices while the right and
  wrong ones announce their state.
- **Reading speed** is inside the card hidden by quiet mode (app.js:918–925), so
  in quiet mode there is no way to set the speed the reader will hear when they
  come out of it.

## What is working, and worth protecting

- The prompt split (`setup` light, `ask` bold) reads well at every size tested,
  and the question card is genuinely calm — no timer, no streak counter, no
  score in view while you think.
- ✓ / ✗ glyphs in their own column mean right and wrong survive without hue, in
  both themes.
- The leave-round guard, the history-per-screen router and the "What slipped"
  list are the right shape; findings 6 and 14 are seams in an otherwise sound
  navigation model.
- "Part of · 3 of 4" is a good compression of the old hundred-word card: one
  line, expandable, in the chapter's own words.
- The reader (sentence player, focus mode, glossary on tap, per-section resume)
  is the strongest screen in the app. The fixes above are largely about getting
  the rest of the app to the standard it already sets.
