Question audit

All 118 questions, read as someone opening the app for the first time: do they know what it’s about, does it stand alone, does it teach, and where does it sit in the bigger picture?

The short answer

Most questions are sound history, but the app asks them in the wrong order and pitches too many at specialists. Only 18 of 118 are anchors: things an ordinary player already knows, like Cortés, potatoes, Cartier, scurvy or the fur trade. The first round today opens with 5 questions that challenge a story the player hasn’t been told yet, starting with how reliable oral history is.

The fix is a ladder: anchor, then deepen, then rethink. Open on what people know, then build on it.

Findings

  1. The opening is back to front.The first round rethinks stories the player hasn’t heard yet. Rethinking works only after the anchor it pushes against.
  2. 18 questions can be answered by picking the most enlightened-sounding option.When the counter-myth is always right, players learn the pattern, not the history. Every rethink question needs one wrong option that also sounds revisionist.
  3. 25 questions use a name or term the player hasn’t met.Pays d’en Haut, Five Nations, Mourning Wars, “virgin soil”, matrilineal, Stadacona. Rule: the first time a name appears, the question says in a clause what it is.
  4. Sources were leaking into questions.“In Belshaw’s view” and similar (17 questions) are fixed in 1.2.1. Rule: the question is about the past, never about the book; the answer card says where it comes from.
  5. 8 questions have options nobody would pick, and 11 test a number rather than an idea.Numbers are kept where the number is the point (fewer than 70 French residents in the 1620s).
  6. Well-known facts that should anchor chapters aren’t asked at all.Columbus, the Norse, Cabot, the Hudson’s Bay Company. The sources state them; see the list below.

The ladder

Anchor

Something the player knows, with one new fact in the question itself.

“Cartier’s crew were dying of scurvy at Stadacona, today’s Quebec City. What saved them?”

Deepen

What was going on behind it, using the anchor they now hold.

“Why did Donnacona try to stop Cartier leaving?”

Rethink

What the school version leaves out, now that it has been set up.

“You may have learned Cartier explored a new land. What does the record show?”
